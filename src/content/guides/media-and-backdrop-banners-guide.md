A media banner is the logo wall guests stand in front of at launches, conferences, graduations and awards nights. The same product is also called a backdrop banner, a step-and-repeat banner or a press wall. Every photo taken in front of it carries your brand, so it keeps working long after the event, on social media, in the press and in company newsletters. This guide covers the sizes we produce, how to choose one and how to design it so it looks right on camera.

## The Three Standard Sizes

We produce media banners in three standard sizes. Each is printed on fabric and fitted to a portable frame. Sizes are width × height.

| Size | Best for | Roughly how many people fit in the photo |
|---|---|---|
| 2.25 m × 2.25 m | Press corners, small launches, photo spots and office events | 2 to 3 |
| 3 m × 2.25 m | Conference registration areas, red-carpet walls and graduations | 3 to 5 |
| 6 m × 2.25 m | Main stages, galas, awards nights and large sponsor walls | Groups and full teams |

All three share the same 2.25 m height, which clears a standing adult with room for logos above head height. If the space calls for something else, we quote a custom size.

## Choosing the Right Size

Start with the photos you want, not the floor space you have.

- **Mostly one or two people at a time,** such as guests arriving or speakers posing: 2.25 m × 2.25 m is enough and fits in tight corners.
- **Small groups and a steady queue,** such as a registration desk or a graduation photo spot: 3 m × 2.25 m gives the photographer room to step back.
- **Full teams, award winners with presenters, or a stage backdrop:** 6 m × 2.25 m keeps the logos visible behind everyone.

Leave about a metre of clear floor in front of the banner for people to stand, and enough space behind it to set up the frame.

## Designing the Logo Pattern

A media banner is read through a camera lens, usually at half-body or full-body crop. That changes the design rules.

1. **Repeat the logos in a tight, even grid.** Whatever the crop, at least one complete logo should appear beside every face. Staggering each row by half a logo helps.
2. **Size logos for the camera.** Logos roughly 25 to 40 cm wide read clearly in a typical photo. Very small logos turn into texture; very large ones get cut off.
3. **Give the host or main sponsor a larger hero logo** at head height in the centre, with the repeat pattern around it.
4. **Use strong contrast.** Dark logos on a white or light background, or white logos on a dark background, photograph best. Avoid fine gradients and thin type.
5. **Keep the bottom 50 cm quiet.** People's legs and furniture cover it in most shots.

Send logos as vector files (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF) so they stay sharp at full size. Our [artwork file requirements](/knowledge-centre/artwork-file-requirements-for-printing/) guide lists what works best.

## Materials, Frames and Glare

We print media banners on fabric because it photographs well under flash, folds into a carry bag without creasing badly and can be reused for many events. Glossy materials reflect camera flashes and stage lights as white patches across the logos, which is the most common reason backdrop photos disappoint.

The fabric fits a portable frame that folds down for transport. A frame of this kind is usually set up by two people without tools. Ask us whether you need a carry bag, spare clips or a replacement print for an existing frame.

## Placement and Lighting

- **Light the front, not the banner.** Soft light on the guests, with no strong lamp aimed straight at the fabric, gives the cleanest photos.
- **Avoid windows behind the camera** if there is direct sunlight; it creates harsh shadows on faces.
- **Place it where people naturally stop,** such as the entrance, the registration desk or beside the stage steps, rather than in a corner they have to find.
- **Mark the standing spot** with tape or a small floor sticker so the photographer gets consistent framing.

## Reusing Your Banner

A media banner built around your brand rather than one event can be used for years: product launches, press briefings, open days and social media shoots. If sponsors change each year, consider a fixed host-logo design for the frame and a lower-cost replacement fabric printed for each event's sponsors.
