A **magic sticker** is the name Kenyan buyers use for a **UV DTF transfer**. It is a full-colour design printed with UV-cured ink onto a clear film. You press the film onto a hard surface, rub it down, and peel the top layer away. The ink stays on the item and the film comes off clean, so the design looks printed directly onto the bottle, glass or laptop, with no sticker edge and no background.

That last part is where the name comes from. The first time most people peel one, the logo seems to appear on the surface by itself.

## How Is a Magic Sticker Different from a Normal Sticker?

A normal vinyl sticker is ink printed on a sheet of plastic. When you stick it on, you are sticking the plastic on too. You can see its edge, feel it with a fingernail, and on clear glass you can see the rectangle or outline of the sticker material around the design.

A magic sticker has no carrier material left on the item. What stays behind is only:

1. A layer of adhesive, shaped exactly like the design.
2. A white ink layer, so colours are opaque on dark or clear surfaces.
3. The colour (CMYK) ink.
4. A clear varnish on top, which gives the slightly raised, glossy finish.

Because there is no sticker body, letters can float free of each other — a company name in separate letters goes on as separate letters, with the surface showing between them.

## What Does "UV DTF" Mean?

**DTF** stands for *direct-to-film*: the design is printed onto a film first, then transferred to the item. **UV** means the ink is cured — hardened — by ultraviolet light the moment it is printed, instead of drying slowly or being fixed with heat.

This is different from the DTF used on t-shirts. Garment DTF uses water-based ink and a powdered adhesive that melts under a heat press into fabric. UV DTF uses rigid UV ink and a pressure-sensitive adhesive, and it is applied cold, by hand. That is why magic stickers suit hard surfaces and garment DTF suits clothing. ## What Surfaces Do Magic Stickers Stick To?

They need a surface that is hard, clean and reasonably smooth:

- glass and ceramic
- stainless steel, aluminium, powder-coated and anodised metal
- acrylic, ABS and PVC plastics
- sealed timber and MDF
- leather and PU (for items that flex but do not stretch)
- stone, tile and canvas board

They do **not** hold reliably on silicone, wax-coated items, heavily textured stone or fabric. Silicone and wax are designed so nothing sticks to them; rough stone traps air under the print; fabric stretches and gets washed, and the cured ink cracks.

## How Long Do Magic Stickers Last?

On items that are hand-washed and used indoors, a well-applied transfer lasts for years. The things that shorten its life are:

- **Dishwashers.** Heat and strong detergent weaken the adhesive over repeated cycles.
- **Sustained heat** above about 60°C, such as microwaves or car dashboards in direct sun.
- **Poor surface preparation.** Grease and dust are the main reason a transfer lifts at the edges.
- **Long outdoor exposure.** Strong sun gradually fades any printed ink.

## Is There a Minimum Order?

Yes. The minimum order is half a metre of film (58 cm wide × 50 cm long); a full metre measures 58 × 100 cm. That half metre can carry many small designs at once, so one order can cover several logos or a run of personalised names. UV DTF needs no screens, plates or setup, and larger orders lower the unit price because more designs fit on each length of film. ## When Is a Magic Sticker the Wrong Choice?

- On clothing, use garment DTF.
- On items going through a dishwasher daily, consider sublimation (white mugs) or laser engraving (steel bottles).
- For very large graphics, over roughly A4, which are hard to apply without trapping air, a cut vinyl decal is easier.
- For thousands of identical single-colour logos on pens, pad printing can be cheaper at that scale.
