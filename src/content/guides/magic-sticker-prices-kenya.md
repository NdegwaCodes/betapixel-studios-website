The honest answer is that a magic sticker's price depends mostly on **how much film it uses**, and then on **how many you order** and **whether you want them applied**. Two quotes for "a magic sticker" can differ several times over because one is a 40 mm logo and the other is a 200 mm wrap.

This article explains what goes into the price, so you can compare quotations properly and brief a printer in a way that gets you an accurate number the first time.

## 1. Film Area: The Biggest Factor

UV DTF is printed on rolls of film. The printer charges, directly or indirectly, for the length of film your job uses. Designs are arranged side by side and nested together to use as little film as possible.

That means:

- **Size matters more than complexity.** A full-colour photo costs about the same as a one-colour logo of the same size, because the ink is a small part of the cost.
- **Shape matters.** A long, thin design (a name across a bottle) wastes less film than a tall one, because it nests better.
- **Many small designs are efficient.** A sheet of forty 30 mm logos uses film better than four 150 mm logos.

When you ask for a price, give the **finished size in millimetres** of each design. "Logo for a bottle" could mean anything from 40 to 120 mm wide.

## 2. Quantity

There is no setup cost for UV DTF (no screens, no plates). The minimum order is half a metre of film (58 cm wide × 50 cm long), and that sheet can hold many designs at once; a full metre is 58 × 100 cm. The price per piece falls as quantity rises, for two reasons:

1. The fixed time spent checking artwork, preparing the file and trimming is shared across more pieces.
2. More copies of the same design nest more tightly on the film.

A fair quote will show a unit price that drops at sensible quantity breaks, for example at 10, 50 and 100 pieces.

## 3. White Ink and Varnish

Magic stickers are printed with a white layer under the colours and usually a clear varnish on top. These are normally included. Ask if a quote seems unusually low — a transfer printed without white will look faint on a dark bottle and disappear on clear glass.

Some jobs deliberately leave out white in places, so the surface shows through the design. That is a design choice, not a saving, and it should be discussed before printing.

## 4. Application

You can apply magic stickers yourself. It needs clean hands, a clean surface and a plastic card. Or the printer can apply them for you.

Application is labour. On a flat item it is quick; on a curved tumbler or a small pen it takes longer and needs more care. A good quotation lists **application as its own line**, so you can decide whether to do it in-house. For a corporate order of 100 bottles, having the printer apply and quality-check every piece is usually worth it.

## 5. The Item Itself

If the printer supplies the bottles, mugs or other items, the price of those blanks usually outweighs the cost of the branding. A stainless steel vacuum bottle costs far more than the transfer on it. Ask for **the blank and the branding priced separately**. If you already have the items, bring them and you pay for the branding only.

## 6. Artwork Preparation

If your logo is print-ready — a vector file with outlined text — there is nothing to charge. If it only exists as a photo, a screenshot or a Word file, it needs to be redrawn before it will print sharply. That is a one-off design charge and you should receive the vector file afterwards for future jobs. Our guide to [artwork file requirements](/knowledge-centre/artwork-file-requirements-for-printing/) explains what "print-ready" means.

## 7. Turnaround and Delivery

Standard turnaround for magic stickers is typically 24 to 48 hours. Same-day work may carry a premium because it interrupts the queue. Delivery within Nairobi and courier to other towns should be a separate, visible line.

## How to Read a Magic Sticker Quotation

A clear quotation from a Kenyan print shop should let you answer these questions without phoning back:

| Line | What it should state |
|---|---|
| Transfers | Size of each design, quantity, unit price |
| Blank items | Item, colour, capacity, unit price (if supplied) |
| Application | Per piece, if requested |
| Artwork | One-off charge, if the file needs redrawing |
| Delivery | Location and cost |
| Turnaround | Working days from artwork approval |

If a quote gives only one total, ask for it itemised. You cannot compare two printers otherwise.
