A mirror is one of the few things in a shop, restaurant or event space that almost everyone looks at, and most people photograph. A customised mirror turns that moment into branding: a selfie mirror printed like a social media post, or a mirror and glass front decorated with frosted-effect designs and your name. This guide covers the main types, where they work best and how to design one that looks good and stays useful.

## Two Main Types

**Printed selfie mirrors.** A full-length mirror with a printed frame around it, often designed to look like a social media post: profile name and photo at the top, likes and comments along the bottom. Guests take a photo of themselves in it, and the frame puts your name and handle in every picture they share. These usually stand on an easel or a floor stand, so they can move between your entrance, a party area or an event.

**Frosted-effect designs on mirrors and glass.** White or frosted designs applied to a mirror, a glass partition or a shopfront: flowers, patterns, a logo or the name of the space. They add decoration and privacy while still letting light through. See our [etched glass frontage](/portfolio/?service=customised-mirrors) for an example on a restaurant terrace.

## Where Customised Mirrors Work Best

- **Restaurants, cafés and lounges:** a selfie mirror near the entrance or bathroom corridor, where guests stop anyway.
- **Salons, spas and boutiques:** branded mirrors at stations double as decoration and a photo spot.
- **Events and launches:** a portable selfie mirror beside the [media banner](/knowledge-centre/media-and-backdrop-banners-guide/) gives guests a second photo moment.
- **Offices and receptions:** frosted designs on glass meeting rooms add privacy and carry the brand through the space.

## Designing a Selfie Mirror Frame

1. **Keep the reflection clear.** The printed frame should sit around the edges; leave the centre free from about knee to head height so people can see themselves.
2. **Put the name and handle where the camera sees it.** The top band and bottom band appear in nearly every phone photo; small details in the corners often get cropped.
3. **Use bold, simple graphics.** The frame is seen at arm's length in a phone screen, so fine text disappears.
4. **Add a reason to share,** such as a hashtag or a short line like "Tag us", so the photos lead back to you.
5. **Match the space.** Black frames suit dark, moody interiors; white frames suit bright cafés and salons.

## Designing Frosted Glass and Mirror Graphics

- **Plan for both sides.** Glass is seen from inside and outside, and some designs read backwards from one side. Text should face the side customers see first.
- **Leave clear bands at eye level** on doors and partitions so people do not walk into the glass, while still showing it is there.
- **Mix open and filled areas.** Flowers, lines and patterns let light through; solid areas give privacy where it is needed.
- **Use your brand sparingly.** One name or logo near the entrance, with the rest decorative, usually looks more premium than a repeated logo.

## Care and Cleaning

Clean mirrors and decorated glass with a soft cloth and a mild glass cleaner, wiping gently over the graphics rather than scrubbing the edges. Avoid scrapers and abrasive pads on decorated areas. Keep portable selfie mirrors on a stable stand away from walkways, and transport them upright and padded.
